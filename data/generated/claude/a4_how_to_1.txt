How to restart a home router

1. Unplug the router's power cable from the wall.
2. Wait a full 30 seconds so the capacitors drain.
3. Plug the cable back in and watch the lights.
4. Wait about two minutes until the internet light turns solid green.
5. Open a web page on your laptop to check the connection.

If the light stays orange, check that the thin cable from the wall socket is firmly clicked in.

Fixing a bicycle puncture on the roadside

Flip the bike upside down onto its saddle and handlebars. Release the brake and open the quick-release lever to remove the wheel. Use two tyre levers to pry one side of the tyre off the rim, then pull out the inner tube. Inflate the tube slightly and listen for the hiss to find the hole. Run your fingers carefully inside the tyre to find any thorn or glass. Fit a new tube, seat the tyre back on the rim, pump to the pressure printed on the sidewall, and refit the wheel.

How to repot a houseplant

Pick a pot that is only 2-3 cm wider than the current one, with drainage holes. Water the plant a day beforehand so the roots slide out easily. Tip the plant on its side and gently ease it out, loosening the root ball with your fingers. Trim any black or mushy roots with clean scissors. Add fresh potting mix to the bottom of the new pot, place the plant at the same depth as before, and fill around the sides. Press lightly, water thoroughly, and keep it out of direct sun for a week.

Changing a flat tyre

1. Park on level ground, switch on the hazard lights, and apply the handbrake.
2. Take the spare wheel, jack and wrench from the boot.
3. Loosen the wheel nuts a half turn while the car is still on the ground.
4. Place the jack under the marked jacking point and raise the car until the wheel clears the ground.
5. Remove the nuts, pull the wheel off, and mount the spare.
6. Tighten the nuts by hand, lower the car, then tighten them in a star pattern.
7. Check the spare's pressure at the next petrol station.

Sharpen a kitchen knife on a whetstone

1. Soak a 1000-grit whetstone in water for 10 minutes.
2. Place the stone on a damp towel so it will not slide.
3. Hold the knife at a 15-degree angle against the stone, edge facing away from you.
4. Push the blade across the stone from heel to tip, with light pressure.
5. Repeat 10 times, then flip the knife and do the other side.
6. Rinse the blade, dry it, and test it on a sheet of paper.

Unclog a kitchen sink without chemicals

First, remove standing water with a cup. Pour a kettle of just-boiled water down the drain. If it is still slow, sprinkle half a cup of baking soda into the drain, then pour in a cup of vinegar and cover the hole with a plug for ten minutes. Rinse with more hot water. For stubborn blockages, place a plunger over the drain with an inch of water in the sink and pump firmly about ten times. Finally, unscrew the U-bend, clean out any debris, and put it back.

Bleeding a radiator

Switch the heating on and wait until the radiators are warm, then identify the one that has cold spots near the top. Turn the heating off. Place a cloth under the bleed valve, then use a radiator key to turn the valve a quarter turn anticlockwise. You will hear hissing air. Close the valve as soon as water starts to spit out. Check the boiler pressure gauge and top it up to 1.2 bar if it has dropped.

Remove a stripped screw

Put a wide rubber band between the screwdriver tip and the screw head, then press down firmly and turn slowly. If it still slips, tap a slightly larger bit into the head with a hammer for a better bite. Another option: cut a new slot across the head with a hacksaw or rotary tool and use a flat screwdriver. As a last resort, drill a small hole into the screw and use a screw extractor, turning anticlockwise.

Sew a button back on

1. Thread a needle with about 50 cm of thread and knot the end.
2. Push the needle up through the fabric from the back at the mark where the button was.
3. Slide the button onto the needle and lay a matchstick across the top.
4. Sew down through the other hole, then up again, around five times.
5. Remove the matchstick and wind the thread around the stitches under the button.
6. Push the needle to the back, tie off and snip the thread.

Replace a ceiling light bulb safely

1. Switch off the light and wait for the bulb to cool.
2. Set a stable step ladder directly beneath the fitting.
3. Twist off any glass cover and put it somewhere safe.
4. Hold the old bulb and turn it anticlockwise until it comes free.
5. Check the wattage and base type on the bulb's label.
6. Screw in the new bulb clockwise until snug; do not overtighten.
7. Refit the cover and test the switch.
