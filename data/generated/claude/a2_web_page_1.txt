<<<ITEM kind=web_page lang=en hard_negative=false topic=energy tariff explained>>>
Understanding Your Fixed-Rate Electricity Tariff

A fixed-rate tariff locks the price per kilowatt-hour for the length of your contract, usually 12 or 24 months. Your standing charge stays the same too, so your bill changes only when you use more or less energy.

Things to check before you sign:
- Early exit fees, which can be as much as 60 EUR per fuel
- Whether the rate includes network charges
- The date your contract ends and when your supplier must notify you

If wholesale prices fall, you will not benefit until the contract ends. Many households still prefer the certainty, especially in winter.

<<<ITEM kind=web_page lang=en hard_negative=false topic=gardening tips>>>
Planting Garlic in October: A Simple Guide

Garlic planted in autumn develops stronger roots and gives larger bulbs the following July. Choose a sunny spot with well-drained soil and dig in a little compost beforehand.

Separate the bulbs into individual cloves, keeping the papery skin on. Plant each clove pointed end up, about 5 cm deep and 15 cm apart, in rows 25 cm apart. Water once and then leave them be; frost will not harm them.

Mulch with straw to suppress weeds. In spring, remove any flower stems (scapes) so the plant puts its energy into the bulb. Harvest when the lower leaves turn brown.

<<<ITEM kind=web_page lang=en hard_negative=false topic=home renovation advice>>>
Before You Knock Down That Wall

Opening up two rooms into one is a popular renovation, but not every wall can come out. Load-bearing walls support the floors above and need a steel beam to replace them. This must be specified by a structural engineer and approved by your local building authority.

Typical costs for a single load-bearing wall removal range from 2,500 to 6,000 EUR, including beam, labour and making good. Non-structural partition walls are much cheaper.

Expect dust, noise and a week or two of disruption. Plan electrical and plumbing changes at the same time to avoid opening walls twice.
