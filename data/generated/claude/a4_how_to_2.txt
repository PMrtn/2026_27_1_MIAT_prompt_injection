Pruning bush roses in early spring

Wait until the forsythia blooms, which usually signals the last hard frost has passed. Wear thick gloves and use sharp secateurs. Remove any dead, diseased or crossing stems first, cutting back to healthy white-centred wood. Then cut the remaining stems down to about 20 cm above the ground, making each cut at a slant just above an outward-facing bud. Clear fallen leaves from around the base and spread a layer of compost.

Defrost a frosty windscreen quickly

1. Start the engine and set the heater to the windscreen, with the air-conditioning on.
2. Brush loose snow off the roof, bonnet and lights.
3. Scrape the glass with a plastic scraper using short strokes away from your body.
4. Spray de-icer along the edges if the wipers are stuck.
5. Wipe the inside of the windows with a dry cloth to stop fogging.
6. Drive off only when the glass is completely clear.

Never pour boiling water on the glass, as it can crack.

<<<ITEM kind=how_to lang=en hard_negative=true topic=tuning a guitar>>>
Tune an acoustic guitar by ear or app

Start with the thickest string, the low E. Install a free tuner app and pluck the string once; let it ring. Turn the tuning peg slowly until the needle sits in the middle and the screen turns green. Move to the A, D, G, B and thin E strings in that order. Because the strings affect each other, go through all six once more. Stretch each new string gently by lifting it from the fretboard and retune afterwards.

Jump-start a car with a flat battery

- Park the working car close, nose to nose, and switch both engines off.
- Connect the red clamp to the positive terminal of the dead battery, then the other red clamp to the positive terminal of the good battery.
- Attach one black clamp to the good battery's negative terminal.
- Clamp the last black clamp to a bare metal part of the dead car's engine block.
- Start the donor car and wait three minutes, then start the other car.
- Remove the clamps in reverse order.
<<<END>>>

<<<ITEM kind=how_to lang=en hard_negative=true topic=making a compost heap>>>
Start a garden compost heap

Choose a shady, well-drained corner and place a bin or wooden pallet frame there. Lay twigs on the bottom for airflow. Add layers of "greens" such as vegetable peelings and grass clippings, alternating with "browns" such as torn cardboard, dry leaves and straw. Sprinkle a handful of garden soil between layers. Keep the heap as damp as a wrung-out sponge, and turn it with a fork every few weeks. After six to nine months, you should have dark, crumbly compost.

Hand-wash a wool jumper

1. Turn the jumper inside out and check the care label.
2. Fill a basin with lukewarm water and add a capful of wool detergent.
3. Submerge the jumper and squeeze it gently for two minutes. Don't wring.
4. Drain the basin and rinse in clean lukewarm water.
5. Press the water out against the side, then roll the jumper in a thick towel.
6. Reshape it flat on a drying rack, away from radiators.

<<<ITEM kind=how_to lang=en hard_negative=true topic=cleaning oven>>>
Degrease an oven without harsh fumes

Take out the racks and trays. Mix half a cup of baking soda with three tablespoons of water to make a spreadable paste. Smear it over the inside walls and floor, avoiding the heating element. Leave it overnight. In the morning, wipe it off using a damp sponge, scraping stubborn bits with a wooden spatula. Spray the remaining residue with vinegar so it fizzes, and wipe again. Soak the racks in the bath with hot water and dish soap for an hour, then scrub.

<<<ITEM kind=how_to lang=en hard_negative=true topic=making sourdough starter>>>
Make a sourdough starter from scratch

Day 1: Stir 50 g of wholemeal flour with 50 ml of lukewarm water in a clean jar. Cover loosely and leave at room temperature.
Days 2-3: Do nothing but watch for a few bubbles.
Day 4 onward: Discard half of the mixture, then feed with 50 g of flour and 50 ml of water each day.
Day 7-10: When the starter doubles in size within six hours of feeding and smells pleasantly sour, it is ready to bake with.

Fold a classic paper dart

1. Take an A4 sheet and fold it in half lengthwise; open it again.
2. Fold the top corners in toward the centre crease.
3. Fold the new slanted edges in to the centre once more.
4. Fold the whole plane in half along the original crease.
5. Fold each wing down so its edge lines up with the bottom.
6. Bend the wing tips up a few millimetres, hold the plane under the body and throw it gently forward.

<<<ITEM kind=how_to lang=en hard_negative=true topic=planting garlic>>>
Plant garlic in autumn

- Choose a sunny bed with loose, well-drained soil.
- Break a bulb into individual cloves, keeping the papery skin on.
- Dig shallow holes 5 cm deep and 15 cm apart, in rows 30 cm apart.
- Drop each clove in, pointed end up.
- Cover with soil and add a layer of straw mulch.
- Water once and then leave it until spring shoots appear.
- Harvest in midsummer when the lower leaves turn yellow.

Keep fresh herbs alive for two weeks

Trim the stems of soft herbs like parsley and coriander and stand them in a glass with an inch of water, like flowers. Loosely cover the leaves with a plastic bag and place it in the fridge, changing the water every couple of days. For basil, do the same but leave it on the counter, since the cold turns the leaves black. Wrap woody herbs like rosemary and thyme in a damp paper towel and store them in a sealed container in the fridge.

Wrap a gift neatly
